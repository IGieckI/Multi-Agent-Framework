Does talk help small LLM teams?

Every experiment of the Multi-Agent Framework study so far: why each one was run, what it ran, what it found, and what it changed. Small open models play a crafting game in teams, under different ways of communicating, and each team is compared with the same agents silenced, with a single agent and with random players.

1. In short

01

When every agent reads every other agent, talking makes the team worse. Six agents with messages and shared results solve 53% of tasks, the same six silenced 62%: −9.4 points [−13.8, −5.0], for every model. Agents herd onto the same combinations.

F16, F17, F24, F26
02

A star (spokes report to a hub, the hub sends a few messages) is neutral, at 3 and at 6 agents, and the cheapest team per solved task. The difference from fully connected is the structure, not the team size.

F18, F24, F29
03

Random players are a strong baseline. Six random agents match six LLM agents on one-step tasks (83% vs 82%) and beat them on two-step tasks (64% vs 41%). The models' knowledge pays off for a single agent and for small teams on one-step tasks only.

F30, F31
04

When the knowledge needed is split between agents, talk does not pool it. With the target's two recipes held by two different agents, teams solve about 22% with or without talk; one agent holding both, 70%; a team where everyone holds both, 91%. Told to share, holders do, but their teammates still rarely act on what they hear: +15 points, still 54 below the team that holds everything.

F33, F34, F37, F38
05

Telling the receivers to use the notes changes nothing, but a bigger model uses them. The 4B models still play a teammate's recipe about one time in ten when told it is true (−5 points). Qwen3-8B pools split notes through talk: +15 to +28 points over its silenced team, and with the share instruction it matches one agent holding both recipes (73% vs 68%). It is no better than Qwen3-4B alone; its whole gain comes through communication.

F42, F43
06

Teams help only by adding independent attempts: one agent given the team's token budget does as well as six silent agents. Varying the temperature adds nothing; sharing failures does not help the models either, because they retry pairs a teammate already reported failing.

F5, F19, F21, F27, F35
07

Agents lose track of their own progress. They remake an intermediate they already hold instead of moving on, and fully connected talk that restates the plan ("let's make explosion first!") locks the whole team into the loop: Qwen3-8B teams where everyone holds both recipes lose 25 points to talk.

2. The setup

The game: WordCraft

WordCraft uses the recipe book of Little Alchemy 2 (700 items). An agent starts with a small inventory and a target. Each round it names two items of its inventory; if the pair is a recipe, the result joins the inventory. Items are never used up, every round counts, and a repeated pair wastes a round. Teams have 4 rounds.

  • Depth 1: the target is one combination away. Depth 2: an intermediate item must be made first.
  • Distractors: extra starting items that are not on the target's path.
  • A team succeeds if any agent makes the target (solved_any). Each agent plays in its own copy of the game; what one makes, the others do not get.

The agents

Open models run locally through Hugging Face transformers (one call at a time), at temperature 1.0, with reasoning off and at most 256 answer tokens. Each answer has the form Combination: 'a' and 'b', then Reasoning:, then, when the agent can talk, Message:. An answer that cannot be read, or that names an item outside the inventory, is refused and asked again (up to 3 times).

The pilot tried 10 models; from the main experiment on, the team used Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B (main only), Gemma 3 4B and Llama 3.2 3B. Two prompt versions exist: v1 and v2 (v2 frames the rules' example as a different game, section 3.2).

Communication protocols

A protocol says who can send what to whom. Two kinds of things travel: results (an automatic note of each valid or invalid combination an agent tried) and messages (the free text after Message:).

How a grid runs

Reading the numbers. Differences are paired (same target, one thing changed) with 95% bootstrap intervals in brackets. With 24 runs per cell an interval spans about ±20 points, with 240 about ±6. Intervals on this page's charts are resampled by target and can differ by a point from those in FINDINGS.md.

3. The experiments, in order

3.1 Pilot

29 – 31 Oct
WhyA first sweep to see which models can play at all, which task settings separate conditions, what a run costs, and whether the pipeline holds up at scale.
Grid
configs/experiments/pilot.yaml, prompts v1
Ran
10 models (Qwen3 0.6/1.7/4/8B, Gemma 3 1/4B, Llama 3.2 1/3B, SmolLM2 360M/1.7B) × isolated, fully_connected, star × depth 1/2 × 0/3 distractors × 6 replicates × base, all_independent, one_agent
Size
2,160 runs, 0 failed; about 20 GPU-hours on L40s

What it found

  • 44% of runs were exact duplicates (one_agent is the same in every protocol; isolated equals fully_connected without links): only 1,200 of 2,160 were distinct (F3). It also showed that runs are reproducible.
  • all_solved was the wrong success measure (every agent must solve), so star looked better than fully_connected only because it has fewer agents. Team success is solved_any from now on (F4).
  • Teams beat one agent by making more attempts, not by talking: one agent 39%, fully connected silenced 52% vs talking 47%, star silenced 54% vs talking 52% (F5).
  • Teams gain more the more different their agents are; Qwen3 agents behave almost like copies (F6).
  • Llama and SmolLM2 copied the rules' example (a "cyborg" made from metal and life) and played the real Little Alchemy ('stone', 'earth') instead of their own inventory: 59–76% of their answers were refused (F8, F12, F13).
  • Gemma 3-4B did not load at first (its config field is nested), fixed (F1). Cost is nearly independent of model size: generation is bound by per-token overhead (F11).
What it changedDuplicate runs are merged automatically; success is solved_any; the next grids use only the two middle settings; a new prompt version was written and checked (3.2); four models were kept.

1 Nov
WhyIn v1 the rules' example was laid out exactly like the real task, under the same header, and weak models took it for their own game. v2 frames it as a different game (<Example task> … <End of example>) and says to use only items of one's own inventory. Nothing else changes.
Grid
configs/experiments/prompt_v2_check.yaml, job 74062
Ran
6 models × fully_connected, star × the two middle settings × 4 replicates, each run on the same target as its pilot twin
Size
144 distinct runs, compared pair by pair with v1

  • SmolLM2 stays unreliable (format failures), so it was dropped.
What it changedv2 replaces v1 for every later experiment; Llama 3.2-3B joins the main grid as a third model family.

3.3 Main experiment

1 Nov
WhyThe pilot hinted that talk never helps. The main experiment tests it properly: four capable models, the two informative settings, 20 replicates, and every fair baseline, including one agent with the team's token budget.
Grid
configs/experiments/main.yaml, jobs 74066/74067 (L40, 6.5 h)
Ran
Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, Gemma 3-4B, Llama 3.2-3B × fully_connected (6), star (3) × (depth 1, 3 distractors), (depth 2, 0 distractors) × 20 replicates × base, all_independent, temperature sweep, one_agent, one_vs_multi_token
Size
1,440 distinct runs, 0 failed; 160 pairs per protocol

What it found

  • Why talk hurts: herding (F17). Agents follow what they receive 87% of the time, 26% of the time with no reason of their own. The team explores less: distinct winning paths 0.54 vs 0.64, more alike inventories, more repeats. Messages pull six agents onto one plan instead of pooling different knowledge.
  • One agent with the team's token budget does better than six talking agents (+6.9 [+0.6, +13.1]) and as well as six silent ones (−2.5); three-agent teams still beat it (F19). It wins partly by playing longer: 17 rounds on average.
  • Talk in fully connected costs 54% more tokens for less success: 27.5k tokens per solved task vs 15.2k silenced (F20).
  • Two baseline flaws found: the temperature sweep averaged 0.85, colder than everyone else's 1.0, so it tested "some colder agents" instead of variety (F21); the token-matched agent was shown 240 "remaining rounds", the safety cap, instead of its budget (F15).
  • A confound: fully connected has 6 agents and star 3, so structure and size were mixed (F22).
What it changedThe follow-up separates size from structure, splits the two channels, centres the temperature sweep on 1.0 and shows the token-matched agent its budget.

3.4 Follow-up

2 – 3 Nov
WhyIs fully connected worse because of its structure or its size? Which channel causes the herding: shared results or messages? And do the corrected baselines change anything?
Grid
configs/experiments/followup.yaml, jobs 74110, 74114 (RTX 2080 Ti, float16), 74115 (L40, bfloat16)
Ran
Qwen3-4B, Gemma 3-4B, Llama 3.2-3B × 6 protocols (fully_connected and star at 3 and 6 agents; fully connected sharing only results or only messages) × the main settings × 20 replicates × the main versions, temperatures (0.7, 1.0, 1.3), budget shown
Size
2,520 distinct runs, 0 failed; 120 pairs per protocol

What it found

  • Runs are reproducible across jobs and nodes: Gemma's repeated runs are byte-identical to the main experiment's; Qwen and Llama in float16 change exact tokens but not outcomes (0.00 [−0.03, +0.03]), so the two experiments can be read together (F23).
  • Talk erases the gain from a bigger team: silenced, 6 agents beat 3 (+5.8 fully connected, +4.2 star); with links, size makes no difference (F25).
  • Shared results get copied (2.6 copy events per run); messages make agents retry each other's suggestions (repeat rate 0.37, the highest) (F26).
  • A temperature sweep centred on 1.0 changes nothing (F27). Showing the token-matched agent its budget changes nothing either (F28).
What it changedThe study had a consistent story: teams help by adding attempts, and talk only gets in the way. Before building more conditions on it, the next step questioned the task itself.

3.5 Random agents

3 – 4 Nov
WhyThe search spaces are tiny: depth 1 has 5 items (15 pairs), depth 2 about 3 items (6 pairs). If blind trial and error solves them, "more attempts" means "more randomness", and the LLMs' knowledge may matter little. First simulated, then run through the real pipeline with random players.
Grid
configs/experiments/random_baseline.yaml, job 74155 (RTX 2080 Ti, CPU work)
Ran
mock and mock_norepeat × 5 protocols × the main settings × 200 targets (0–19 are the main ones) × base, all_independent, one_agent, one_agent_scaled_steps
Size
9,600 distinct runs, 0 failed
  • The LLMs explore badly: 41% of their depth-2 rounds repeat a pair they already tried, against the rules, and agents of one model try the same pairs. The outcome is mostly fixed by the target: silent teams of 6 and of 3 have the same outcome on 148 of 160 targets.
  • Sharing failures is what helps random teams: +16.5 points at depth 2 (80.5% vs 64.0%). This was the room left for a talk design to win back.
  • Shortcut targets (F32). The generator does not check a target's other recipes: 51 of 200 depth-2 targets can be made in one step from the starting items (3 of the 20 used). They carry much of the depth-2 success (six silent agents: 100% on them, 31% on the rest). The paired results hold without them: F16 becomes −6.6 [−11.0, −2.2].
What it changedRandom agents became part of the framework and a baseline for every later grid. And since every agent saw the same inventory and target, talk had nothing to carry except who tries what: the next experiment gives agents different information.

3.6 Hints: agents that know different things

4 – 5 Nov
WhyThe first test where talk has something useful to carry. Each agent gets two private notes, true recipes only it can see. With notes split, the target's two recipes sit with two different agents and every other note is a true recipe off the path (a decoy); with all, every agent holds both; with none, nobody has notes. A bigger task (depth 2, 6 distractors: 9 items, 45 pairs) keeps random teams near 12%.
Grid
configs/experiments/hints.yaml, jobs 74158, 74161 (gates), 74162 (RTX 2080 Ti), 74163 (L40)
Ran

  • It fails at both ends: holders rarely tell, and seldom even use their own notes; the others rarely act on what they hear. The models do not reason "my note makes X, and the target needs X".
  • Even one agent holding both recipes never tries the intermediate's recipe in 11 of 48 runs. With every agent holding both, 275 of 276 refused answers try the target's recipe before the intermediate exists.
  • Sharing only failures does not help the models (+2.1 at best): with a teammate's failure in front of them, 20% of their moves retry that same pair (F35).

What it changedThe generator can now reject shortcut targets (task.allow_shortcuts: false; old grids keep theirs). The next grid asks whether the models do not think of telling, or cannot use what they are told.

What it changedThe bottleneck is the receiving side: these models do not act on true information from teammates. Next candidates: a stronger model or an instruction to the receivers on the same targets, and teams mixing model families (to test F6).

3.8 Told to use, and a bigger model

4. Caveats and corrections

  • Small tasks and few targets. 3–9 starting items, 4 rounds, 20 targets per setting (40 in hints2). Effects under about 10 points cannot be seen (about 8 in hints2).
  • Small models, reasoning off, one prompt wording, and personas still from v1. The one 8B result (F43) rests on one model and 40 targets; reasoning models are untested.
  • Shortcut targets (F32) inflate every depth-2 success rate before hints2 (hints2 has none). Paired differences hold without them.
  • An overclaim corrected: earlier notes said variety between model families helps (F6). No team mixing models has been run yet (queued on 7 Nov); F6 only shows that models whose agents differ more gain more from a team.
  • A typo corrected: F18's pooled star value read −1.3 (the depth-1 number); it is +1.2 [−4.4, +6.9]. The conclusion is the same.
