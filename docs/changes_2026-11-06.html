The short version. Communication between small open LLM agents has not helped on WordCraft in any setting tested so far. Fully connected talk makes teams worse (about −10 points at 6 agents) because the agents herd onto the same combinations; a star is neutral. Teams beat one agent only through more independent attempts, and random agents do that better. When the two recipes of the target are split between two agents, talk recovers almost none of the gap to an agent that holds both. Telling holders to share their notes makes them share them (+13 to +15 points), but their teammates still rarely act on what they hear, even when told to (F42). Qwen3-8B is the exception: it pools split notes through talk (+15 to +28 points) and, told to share, matches one agent holding both recipes (F43).

The project

A framework for running teams of small open LLM agents (Qwen3, Gemma 3, Llama 3.2, SmolLM2; 0.36B–8B) on WordCraft, a Little Alchemy 2 environment: each round an agent combines two items of its inventory to make new ones, and must reach a target item within a few rounds (4 steps here). A team plays under a communication protocol (isolated, fully connected, star with a hub and spokes, and variants that share only results, only messages or only failures). Each team is compared with baselines derived from the same config: the same agents with no links, a temperature sweep, one agent, one agent with the team's step or token budget.

Research question: does communication between agents help, and when?

Every version, protocol and model of a replicate gets the same target, so all comparisons are paired. Success is solved_any (any agent reached the target). Intervals are 95% paired bootstraps resampled by target.

Pilot

When 29 – 31 OctGrid 10 models × isolated / fully_connected / star × depth 1–2 × distractors 0/3 × 6 reps × 3 versionsPrompts v1Data run_56/

What it found

  • Gemma 3-4B did not load (a config field in the multimodal config); fixed and rerun F1. The loaded revision was not recorded offline; fixed later F2.
  • 44% of runs were exact duplicates of others (one_agent across protocols, isolated = no-links version). Runs are byte-reproducible F3.
  • ended_by: all_solved means every agent solved; team success must use solved_any F4.
  • Teams beat one agent (39% → 47–54%) through more attempts; messages never helped F5.
  • Teams gain more when their agents differ more: Qwen3 agents behave almost like copies F6.
  • Model ranking (one agent): Gemma 3-4B 62%, Qwen3-8B 54%, Qwen3-4B 50% … Llama 3.2-3B 17% F7.
  • Llama and SmolLM2 refused 59–76% of answers: they copied the rules' example (“metal and human make a cyborg”), played the real Little Alchemy (“stone”, “earth”), or broke the format F8, F12, F13.
  • SmolLM2 barely writes messages F9; two of the four settings sit at the ceiling or the floor F10; about 20 GPU-hours, nearly independent of model size F11.
Takeaway The pipeline works and is reproducible. It led to prompt v2, run deduplication, the use of solved_any, and the two middle task settings for the main grid.

Prompt v2 check

When 1 Nov, job 74062Grid 6 models, v2 vs the pilot's v1 on the same 144 targetsData run_62/

What it found

  • Refused answers of the five weak models fell from 56% to 37%; Llama 3.2-3B from 59% to 22%. Copying the example almost disappeared (“cyborg” in 0 of 323 Llama 3B calls) F14.
  • Llama 3.2-3B success 38% → 54%, close to Qwen3-4B's 62% on the same targets; Qwen3-4B unchanged; calls per run fell (Llama 3B 24 → 13.5).
  • SmolLM2 stays unreliable (format failures remain).
Takeaway v2 replaces v1 for every later grid, and Llama 3.2-3B becomes the third model family. SmolLM2 is dropped.

Main experiment

When 1 Nov, jobs 74066/74067Grid Qwen3-4B, Qwen3-8B, Gemma 3-4B, Llama 3.2-3B × fully_connected (6) / star (3) × 2 settings × 20 reps × 5 versionsData run_main/

What it found

  • Fully connected talk makes the team worse: −9.4 points [−14.4, −5.0], for every model and both settings F16.
  • Why: the agents herd. They adopt 87% of what they receive (26% blindly), explore fewer distinct paths and repeat more F17.
  • In a star, talk neither helps nor hurts: +1.2 [−4.4, +6.9]; the hub writes few messages F18.
  • One agent with the team's token budget matches six silenced agents and beats six talking ones (+6.9); three agents still beat it F19.
  • Fully connected talk costs 54% more tokens for less success: 27.5k tokens per solve vs 15.2k silenced F20.
  • The temperature sweep (mean 0.85) lowered variety instead of raising it F21. The token-matched agent was shown a 240-round cap instead of its budget F15.
  • Structure and team size were confounded (6 vs 3 agents) F22.
Takeaway The study's clearest result: everyone-reads-everyone talk hurts by pulling six agents onto one plan. The follow-up was designed to separate structure from size and find the channel that causes it.

Follow-up

When 2 – 3 Nov, jobs 74110, 74114, 74115Grid Qwen3-4B, Gemma 3-4B, Llama 3.2-3B × 6 protocols × 2 settings × 20 reps × 5 versionsHardware Qwen and Llama on RTX 2080 Ti (float16), Gemma on L40Data run_followup/

What it found

  • Runs are byte-identical across jobs and nodes; float16 on the RTX 2080 gives the same outcomes as bfloat16 F23.
  • It is the structure, not the size: fully connected talk hurts at 6 and 3 agents, star is neutral at both F24.
  • Talk erases the gain from a bigger team: silenced, 6 agents beat 3 by +4 to +6; with links, size makes no difference F25.
  • Herding comes through both channels, roughly additively, more through messages: shared results get copied, messages make agents retry each other's suggestions F26.
  • A temperature sweep centred on 1.0 changes nothing F27; showing the token-matched agent its budget changes nothing F28.
  • The 3-agent star is the cheapest team per solve (9.2k tokens); fully connected 6 with talk costs 26.9k F29.
Takeaway The herding of fully connected teams is a property of the structure, and both kinds of shared content feed it. Temperature variety is not a useful lever.

Random-agent calibration and random_baseline

When 3 Nov (simulation), 4 Nov (cluster, job 74155)Grid mock, mock_norepeat × 5 protocols × 2 settings × 200 reps × 4 versionsData run_random_baseline/

What it found

  • Random teams match LLM teams at depth 1 and beat every LLM condition at depth 2 (6 agents: 64–67% vs 41% silenced, 31% talking) F30, F31.
  • The LLMs' knowledge picks a good first pair (one agent at depth 1: 65% vs 26% random), but they explore badly: 41% of their depth-2 rounds repeat a pair they already tried.
  • Sharing helps random teams: skipping teammates' failures lifts them from 64% to 80.5% at depth 2.
  • The pipeline adds nothing of its own: mock ignores messages and its base equals its silenced version exactly.
  • A quarter of depth-2 targets have a one-step shortcut from the starting items, which inflates depth-2 success rates (one agent: 92% on them vs 19% on the rest). The paired differences hold without them (F16: −6.6 instead of −9.4) F32.
Takeaway Blind enumeration is a strong baseline on these small search spaces: every result must be read against it. Since all agents see the same inventory and target, talk had nothing to carry except who tries what, which motivated a task where agents hold different information.

Hints: agents that hold different information

When 4 – 5 Nov, jobs 74158, 74161–74163Grid Qwen3-4B, Gemma 3-4B, Llama 3.2-3B, mock_norepeat × 5 protocols (+ failures only) × depth 2, 6 distractors × notes split / all / none × 20 repsData run_hints/

Each agent gets two private notes (true recipes). With notes split, the target's two path recipes go to two different agents and the other notes are decoys off the path; with all, every agent holds both.

What it found

  • Talk does not pool what only some agents know: with notes split, teams solve about 25% with or without talk; one agent holding both recipes solves 60%; a team where everyone holds both ~90% F33.
  • Why, from the logs: holders name their path note in only 6–32% of messages, sometimes do not even try it themselves, and teammates rarely act on what they hear. Decoy notes take 27–40% of all rounds F34.
  • Sharing only failures does not help LLM agents: 20% of their actions repeat a pair a teammate reported failing, while random teams gain 16.5 points from the same information F35.
  • Without notes, talk still hurts (fully connected −10); with full notes it does not help F36.
Takeaway The first test where talk had something to carry, and it carried almost nothing. It fails at both ends: holders do not tell, receivers do not use.

Hints 2 and hints2_share: why the notes are not pooled

When 5 Nov, jobs 74189, 74192–74196Grid the same 4 models × fully_connected, star × notes split with / without decoys, all × 40 targets without shortcuts; plus split fully connected teams told to share their notesData run_hints2/, run_hints2_share/

What it found

  • On 40 targets without shortcuts, F33 holds: talk − silenced 0.0 (fully connected) and +3.3 (star) with notes split; the gap to one agent holding both is 45–48 points F37.
  • Told to share, holders tell; teammates still rarely use it: one sentence makes holders name their recipe in their first message (13% → 63–82%) and adds +13 to +15 points, but a receiver with the intermediate's recipe in its inbox plays it only 16% of the time. Teams stay 46–54 points below a team where everyone holds the notes F38.
  • Decoy notes cost about 10 points; told to share, holders pass on their decoys too (57% of messages) F39.
  • When every agent holds both recipes, talk hurts: −8.3 (fully connected), −6.7 (star). Even one agent holding the whole recipe fails 30% of the time F40.
  • The runs record a dirty checkout, most likely the untracked Slurm log lists on giano; now git-ignored F41.
Takeaway Of the two failures, “do not think of telling” is fixed by one sentence; “do not use what is told” is not. These models do not act on true information from teammates.

Told to use, and a bigger model: hints2_use, hints2_8b, hints2_8b_share

When 6 Nov, jobs 74232–74240Grid hints2_share's teams also told to use teammates' notes (3 models); hints2's cells and the share / use teams with Qwen3-8BData run_hints2_use/, run_hints2_8b/, run_hints2_8b_share/

What it found

  • Telling receivers to use teammates' notes changes nothing: −5.0 [−12.5, +3.3] for the 4B models, −5.0 for Qwen3-8B. A 4B receiver just told the intermediate's recipe plays it at its next move 10% of the time F42.
  • Qwen3-8B pools split notes through talk, the first setting where talk helps: +27.5 [+12.5, +45.0] fully connected, +25.0 star. Its holders tell unprompted (57% name the target recipe in their first message, 4B: 13%) and its receivers act (40% play the intermediate recipe next, 4B: 15%). Alone or silenced it is no better than Qwen3-4B F43.
  • Agents remake an intermediate they already hold, and fully connected talk locks the team into it: with everyone holding both recipes, Qwen3-8B loses 25 points to talk; in all 11 lost targets every action remakes the intermediate F44.
  • The runs record a clean checkout: F41 is closed.
Takeaway The receiving failure is a limit of the 4B models, not of the instructions. A bigger model uses what it is told; whether that is its size or this model is the next test.

Conclusions so far

  • Talk has helped only Qwen3-8B with split notes (F43). For the smaller models fully connected talk hurts (−7 to −11 points) at 3 and 6 agents, because agents herd through both shared results and messages. A star is neutral and the cheapest team per solve.
  • Teams help only through more independent attempts, and talk erases even that gain. One agent with the team's token budget matches six silenced agents.
  • Random agents are a strong baseline: they match LLM teams at depth 1 and beat them at depth 2. The models' knowledge pays off for one agent and small teams on one-step tasks.
  • Split knowledge is not pooled by the 4B models. Holders seldom tell unless told to; receivers seldom act on what they are told even when told it is true (F42).
  • Agents lose track of their own progress: they remake an intermediate they hold, and talk that restates the plan makes it worse (F44).
  • Method: runs are byte-reproducible, duplicates are run once, shortcut targets are excluded in new grids, and every comparison is paired by target.
