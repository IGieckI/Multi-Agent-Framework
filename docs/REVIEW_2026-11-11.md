# Critical review of the multi-agent communication project (2026-11-11)

Scope: HANDOFF.md, FINDINGS.md (F1–F49), `docs/analysis/`, the framework code (engine, bus, WordCraft task, prompts,
protocols) and spot-checks on `runs.csv` and `events.jsonl` of main, followup, hints2 and its 8B / 12B / share grids.
Every number marked "recomputed" was recomputed in this review from the raw files, with the paired bootstrap of
`lib.boot` clustered by target plus an exact sign test on the discordant pairs. The new results are logged as
F50–F54 in FINDINGS.md.

## If you only do 3 things

1. **Fix two engine behaviours that bias the pooling results, then rerun the share cells** (about 2 GPU-hours).
   The engine throws away an agent's message whenever its action is refused, and the agent most often refused is the
   holder of the target's recipe, who tries it before the intermediate exists. Under the share instruction this
   drops 53% of Gemma3-4B's and 29% of Qwen3-4B's messages that name the target's recipe (F50). The message list is
   also cumulative, with no round numbers and no deduplication, so "Let's make explosion first!" from round 1 still
   reads as current in round 4.
2. **Make the private information really private and separate the two channels** (about 10 GPU-hours). Today the
   "split" manipulation leaks: a silenced Gemma3-12B team solves 50–75% of split tasks because the holder of the
   intermediate's recipe guesses the target's recipe from what it knows (F52), and in fully connected teams the
   intermediate's recipe reaches the others through the auto-shared results, not through talk (F51). Run renamed
   (nonsense-word) recipes, and results-only / messages-only protocols for Qwen3-8B and Gemma3-12B.
3. **Before writing, test whether the remake loop is caused by the prompt's example** (about 5 GPU-hours) and
   restate every per-model claim with exact tests. The one-shot example shows exactly "make the intermediate now,
   combine it next round", the agents have no memory of their previous turn, and 52% of Qwen3-8B's remakes repeat the
   example's "in the next round" (F53). If a second example removes the loop, F40, F44, F47 (−38.8) and F48 are
   properties of this prompt, not of talk.

## A. Critical review

### A1. What reproduces

Every headline number I recomputed matches FINDINGS.md to the decimal: F16 (−9.4 [−13.8, −5.0]), F18, F24, F26, the
per-model hints2 contrasts, F43/F47 (Qwen3-8B +32.5 / +25.0 / −38.8 / +13.8), F46 (Gemma3-12B +40.0, −22.5) and the
share contrasts. The pipeline is careful: paired targets, deduplicated runs, byte-reproducibility, a random-agent
baseline run through the real code, shortcut targets found and excluded. That is better practice than most published
multi-agent LLM papers. The problems below are about interpretation, not arithmetic.

### A2. Statistical issues

- **Effective sample size is the number of targets, and it is small.** Main and followup share the same 40 targets
  (20 per setting); the followup is not an independent replication of F16: 566 of 600 shared cells have the same
  outcome (Gemma's runs are byte-identical). "−9.4, then −10.8 again" is one result, not two.
- **Few discordant pairs carry each effect.** F16 rests on 17 discordant pairs of 160 (1 for talk, 16 against), on 16
  distinct targets. It is significant pooled (exact p = 0.0003), but no single model is: Gemma 1/6 (p = 0.13), Llama
  0/2 (0.50), Qwen3-4B 0/5 (0.06), Qwen3-8B 0/3 (0.25). "Negative for every model" is true of the sign only.
- **Percentile bootstrap intervals are too narrow with so few discordant pairs.** Several intervals that "exclude 0"
  fail an exact test: Qwen3-4B star +10 [+2.5, +20] is 4/0 (p = 0.13); Llama share +10 [+2.5, +20] is 4/0 (p = 0.13);
  Gemma3-12B everyone-holds-both fully connected −10 [−20, −2.5] is 0/4 (p = 0.13); F40 per model is 2/5, 2/6, 0/3.
  Rule of thumb: with 40 targets a per-model contrast needs about 6/0 or 10/2 discordant pairs.
- **Multiple comparisons.** 49 findings, several hundred contrasts, models × protocols × settings, all read after
  the fact, none corrected. F18 already says "one positive cell in eight is what chance gives"; the same discipline
  is missing in F45 (per-model star effects, channel effects), F49 (differences in differences) and F39 per model.
  Treat everything except the large effects below as exploratory and confirm on fresh targets (replicates 80+).
- **One sample per target and condition.** A "replicate" is a new target, so target difficulty and sampling noise
  cannot be separated; a discordant pair may be sampling noise. Three or four seeds on the same 40 targets would cost
  the same as 120–160 targets and would say how much of the outcome is fixed by the target (F30 suggests most).
- **Nested conditions.** Silenced fully_connected3 is literally the first three agents of silenced fully_connected
  (same seeds, same prompts: 0 targets where 3 solve and 6 do not). "6 beats 3" (F25) is ≥ 0 by construction and
  measures the marginal value of agents 4–6, which is fine, but it is not two independent samples.
- **Ceiling and floor.** Everyone-holds-both silenced is 87.5–100%, so talk can only hurt there. Gemma3-12B split
  without decoys starts at 75% silenced: share +2.5 says nothing. Hints without notes was at 2–10% (floor).

### A3. Confounds and prompt artifacts

- **Messages are dropped when the action fails** (F50, new; `engine.run_agent` sets `turn.message` only when the
  answer parsed and `apply` returns before publishing on a no-op). The holder of the target's recipe often plays it
  too early ("not in your inventory"), fails three times, and its message never leaves. Dropped share of its
  messages naming its recipe, share instruction, with / without decoys: Gemma3-4B 53% / 27%, Qwen3-4B 29% / 17%,
  Qwen3-8B 14% / 8%, Llama 8% / 6%, Gemma3-12B 1% / 2%. This is correlated with model size, so it inflates the
  "bigger models pool" contrast, and it biases F38's tell rates (computed on published messages only).
- **The result channel does much of the pooling** (F51, new). In fully connected teams every successful combination
  is broadcast as "Other players' valid combinations: 'a' and 'b' -> 'x'". The holder of the target's recipe that
  plays the intermediate's recipe had usually seen it there: Gemma3-12B 12 of 12 after a result and no message at
  all; Qwen3-8B 14 result only, 17 both, 1 message only. F43's explanation ("its holders tell") is half the story,
  and `k4.py` / `k6.py` only track messages. This also gives the cleanest "use" measure, because a result is ground
  truth and needs no trust: of target holders that saw the intermediate's recipe, those who ever played it are
  Gemma3-4B 7% (4/61), Llama 37%, Qwen3-4B 46%, Gemma3-12B 47%, Qwen3-8B 65%.
- **The split manipulation leaks** (F52, new). In silenced split teams the solver is mostly the holder of the
  *intermediate's* recipe (Gemma3-4B 9/12 and 14/19, Gemma3-12B 14/20 and 20/30, Qwen3-4B 7/9 and 7/10): it makes the
  intermediate from its note and guesses the target's recipe. Little Alchemy 2 recipes are on the web and "follow
  real-world intuition", so the second note is partly in the weights, more so in bigger models. Size, family and
  recall of the game are confounded in F43 and F46.
- **The remake loop looks example-driven** (F53, new). The prompt is rebuilt every turn with no memory of the
  agent's own last answer; the example answer is "I make 'robot' now and combine it with 'human' in the next round";
  notes list the intermediate's recipe; the made item is appended at the end of the inventory. A single Qwen3-8B
  agent remakes in 92 of 143 turns after it holds the intermediate, and 48 of those answers say "next round". F48
  (the made line fixes Qwen only) fits a salience account as well as a state-tracking one.
- **Stale, duplicated social context.** Messages accumulate without round labels and results without
  deduplication (one prompt lists the same valid combination ten times). Herding (F17), the lock-in of F44 and the
  −38.8 of F47 are measured under this presentation. A "latest round only" or round-stamped list is an untested
  alternative explanation for "fully connected talk hurts".
- **Star versus fully connected differs in five things**, not one: topology, personas (the hub is told to give each
  spoke something different, fully connected agents get no role), who may write (only the hub: spokes have no
  message slot, so a spoke can never tell its note), what is shared (results up, messages down) and the schedule
  (the hub plays after seeing the round's results). F24 removes the size confound only. "It is the structure" should
  read "it is the star protocol as configured". With notes split, the hub happens to be a holder in 32 of 40 targets.
- **A false statement in the prompt.** "Your notes (true, and only you were told them)" is shown in the
  everyone-holds-both condition, where it is false and invites agents to announce the plan to each other (the F44
  messages).
- **Decoding.** Every model samples at temperature 1.0 with top_p 1 and top_k 0, thinking off, 256 tokens. This is
  outside the recommended settings of Qwen3 and Gemma 3. Repeat rates, parse failures, "random agents explore
  better" and "Qwen agents are copies" may shift under each model's recommended sampling. One robustness cell is
  needed before any claim about a family.
- **Precision and hardware** differ by model (float16 on rtx2080 for Qwen3-4B and Llama, bfloat16 on L40 for the
  rest). F23 makes this a minor worry.
- **Tiny search spaces.** Depth 2 with 0 distractors has about 6 pairs; six random agents enumerate it. F30/F31 are
  right, but they say the main-grid task cannot reward communication at all, which limits what F16–F29 can mean.

### A4. Metric choices

- `solved_any` grows with team size by itself and hides who solved. For pooling, report also the solver's role
  (holder of which recipe, non-holder) and the number of agents that reach the target; they changed the reading of
  three findings here.
- "The team matches one agent holding both" is a weak bar: one agent has 4 attempts, the team 24. The ceiling for a
  team is everyone-holds-both silenced. Against it, Qwen3-8B with share is still −12.5 / −13.8 (recomputed), Gemma3-12B
  −7.5 / −17.5.
- Herding metrics (`adoption_rate`, `blind_adoption_rate`, `copy_events`) are defined on pair overlap; in small
  inventories overlap happens by chance. They need the silenced team's value as a baseline in every table.
- The tell measure (`mentions`: both ingredients as substrings) has a false-positive rate of 3–10% (non-holders'
  round-1 messages), small next to the effects.

### A5. Strongest and weakest findings

Strongest (large, replicated across models or targets, mechanism visible in the logs):

1. **F30/F31**: random agents match or beat LLM teams in the main grid. Simple, robust, and a caution for the field.
2. **F33/F37**: 4B teams with split notes gain nothing from unprompted talk (0 to +3 pooled, 120 pairs), while the
   same information in every head gives +67. Holds whatever the mechanism caveats.
3. **F38 + F42**: one sentence makes holders tell; an explicit instruction to use changes nothing. The asymmetry is
   the most publishable idea here, and F51 strengthens it (observed results go unused too).
4. **F47**: Qwen3-8B on 80 targets, +32.5 (27/1) and −38.8 (1/32). Large and clean, but one model.
5. **F16** pooled: fully connected talk costs about 9 points. Significant, modest, one set of 40 targets.

Weakest:

- **F45** per-model reversals, **F49** talk effects of mixed teams, **F18** "star is neutral" as a structural claim,
  **F39** per-model decoy costs, **F25** as worded: small n, post hoc, uncorrected.
- **F46** "size versus family": one model per cell, with the leak (F52) and the dropped messages (F50) both
  correlated with size.
- **F44/F48** as claims about agents losing track of state: likely prompt-specific (F53).
- **F6/F21/F27** on variety: the temperature sweep at these settings is not a test of diversity in general.

Interesting enough to publish, once cleaned: the tell-versus-use asymmetry with the two-channel decomposition; the
hidden-profile result with a no-prior task; plan lock-in under broadcast talk if it survives the example ablation;
the random baseline as a methodological point.

## B. A coherent story

Five claims the data support, in the order a paper could make them.

1. **When all agents see the same thing, talk has nothing to carry and broadcast talk mis-coordinates search.**
   Teams help only by adding attempts; random agents add more (F30, F31); fully connected talk costs about 9 points
   through convergence on the same pairs (F16, F17, F26). This is the motivation, not the contribution.
2. **Pooling distributed knowledge is a pipeline of two steps, tell and use, and they fail independently.**
   Small models neither tell (13% of first messages) nor use. Telling is a prompting problem: one sentence takes it
   to 63–100%. Using is not: an instruction changes nothing (F42), and even ground-truth results from teammates go
   unused (F51: Gemma3-4B 7%). This is the core claim.
3. **Using what one is told depends on the model; it appears between 4B and 8–12B.** Qwen3-8B and Gemma3-12B act on
   teammates' information 47–92% of the time and their teams approach the everyone-knows ceiling (F43, F46, F47).
   Whether this is capacity or recall of the game is open (F52).
4. **Which channel carries what.** Observed results carry the intermediate's recipe; messages carry the target's
   recipe (the one that cannot be demonstrated before the intermediate exists). Telling unprompted is a Qwen trait
   (F46). This decomposition is new in this review and needs the channel ablation.
5. **Broadcast talk amplifies individual errors into team errors.** One agent's loop (remaking the intermediate)
   becomes the team's plan under fully connected talk (−38.8), while a hub that sees results moves the team on
   (+13.8), and a state cue removes it for Qwen (F44, F47, F48). Conditional on the example ablation.

How they connect: herding (1) and lock-in (5) are the same mechanism, convergence on whatever is most salient in a
shared context; split notes (2–4) are the one setting where convergence could be useful, and there the bottleneck
moves from the channel to the receiver. Size and family effects are modifiers of (2)–(5), not a separate claim, and
with one model per size and family they should be reported as such.

Missing for a solid paper or chapter:

- a task without prior knowledge of the recipes (C3 below);
- the engine fixes and a rerun of the affected cells (C1, C5);
- at least two models per size class, or an honest "case studies of five models" framing;
- confirmatory runs: a short list of pre-stated contrasts on fresh targets, with exact tests;
- a horizon long enough that pooling is not a race (4 rounds leave one round of slack for a two-step exchange);
- a fair topology comparison (C7), or dropping the structural claim;
- one decoding-robustness cell;
- related work: hidden profiles, network structure and exploration, information cascades (section D).

## D. Beyond the current setup

### D1. Communication mechanisms to try

- **Typed messages.** The framework already has typed channels (`typed_channels.yaml`). A `claim` type with fields
  `{recipe, source: note | observed | guess}` separates "I know" from "I think"; receivers get a table, not prose.
  Tests whether the use failure is parsing free text or acting on it. Cheap: a parser for one more answer label.
- **Blackboard / shared memory.** One deduplicated list of known-true recipes that every agent reads (and writes
  through typed claims), replacing the message stream. Removes stale talk and repetition by construction; the
  comparison blackboard versus broadcast at equal information is the cleanest test of claim 5.
- **Ask, do not only tell.** Give agents a `request` type ("who knows how to make X?"). In hidden-profile studies
  questions pull unshared information better than free discussion.
- **Role specialization.** Tell agents who holds what kind of knowledge ("agent_02 has a note about the target").
  This is the expert-role manipulation that improves pooling in human groups (Stasser, Stewart and Wittenbaum 1995).
- **Theory of mind prompts.** Before the message, one line "What do the others not know that I know?". Costs
  tokens; compare with the share instruction, which hard-codes the answer.
- **Communication budget.** A cap on message tokens per agent or per run (the budget code exists). Hypothesis: a
  tight budget reduces herding and raises the share of messages that carry notes.
- **Debate / verify.** A second agent checks a claimed recipe against its own observations before the team adopts
  it (`debate_judge.yaml` exists). Targets the "unfounded claims are followed" example in F38.
- **Summaries.** The summarizer channel (`form: summary`) is implemented and unused: a hub that summarizes
  everyone's notes is a learned-protocol baseline without training.
- **Evolved protocols.** Search over the share sentence and the message format with the solve rate on the renamed
  task as fitness (20–30 candidate prompts × 40 targets is affordable). A small, honest version of "learned
  protocols" for models that cannot be fine-tuned here.
- **Own-turn memory.** Agents that see their previous reasoning (a scratchpad line). Probably removes the remake
  loop; worth one cell because every real agent system has it.

### D2. Tasks that separate "more attempts" from "pooling"

- **Renamed WordCraft** (C3): the minimal change.
- **Deeper split chains.** Depth 3 with three holders: success needs two hand-offs; random and silenced teams
  stay at 0.
- **Classic hidden profile.** Choose the best of three options; shared clues favour the wrong one, unshared clues
  together favour the right one. Scored on the decision, so attempts do not help.
- **Constraint puzzles with distributed clues** (who lives where): each agent holds some constraints; only the
  union determines the answer.
- **Distributed retrieval / multi-hop QA** with the documents split between agents.
- **Cooperative games with private observations** (Hanabi-like): communication is the task.
